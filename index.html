The plant looks a little droopy.

Partner A: What do you think the plant needs? Partner B: What evidence are you using?

Discuss together

  1. Which choices supported growth? Which tried to control growth?
  2. When did “more help” become less helpful?
  3. At what point did you realize that acting quickly was not the same as understanding what was needed?
  4. What might this suggest about the counselor’s role?

Connecting the simulation to Person-Centered Theory

Plant’s capacity for growthActualizing tendency
Light, water, space, timeFacilitating conditions
Trying to pull the plant upwardTrying to impose change from outside
Overhelping or mismatched helpMore intervention is not automatically better intervention
Observing this plant carefullyUnderstanding the person from their frame of reference
Different needs and responsesContext and individual differences matter
Key idea: You did not manufacture growth. You influenced the conditions in which growth could occur.
Metaphor limit: clients are not passive plants. People have agency, culture, histories, relationships, and social contexts. The metaphor is useful specifically for Rogers’ idea that growth is facilitated rather than imposed.
